How this works, and how accurate it is

The formula

iRacing scores safety as Corners Per Incident (CPI). The displayed SR is a mapping of that hidden CPI:

SR = band + 1.5236 · ln( CPI / (3.4949 · 1.4562^(class_index + band)) )

This was fitted to 1,280 real (SR, CPI, licence level) observations taken from iRacing's own results data — MAE 0.007 SR. The band floors form a single geometric ladder, which is why C@3.00 = B@2.00 = A@1.00 = 22.9 CPI.

Why crossing 2.00 or 3.00 jumps

iRacing documents “an additional 0.40 Safety Rating” when you cross a whole number. That is not a bonus bolted on — it falls out of the overlapping bands above. The exact value is 1 − a·ln(r) = 0.427; iRacing rounds it to 0.40 in its help article. The same geometry explains why a class promotion costs almost exactly 1.00 SR.

How one session moves your CPI

Your accumulated CPI is an exponentially weighted moving average over corners. A session of c corners carries weight

alpha  = 1 - exp(-c / window)
window = 1050 x 1.4142^(band-1)   ->  1050 / 1485 / 2100 / 2970 corners

Two things here were measured, not assumed. The window depends only on your whole-number SR band, never on your licence class — a Class B and a Class A driver sitting in the same band share the same window to within 2%. And letting each band take its own free value converges onto exactly that √2 progression. A higher SR means a longer memory, which is why SR gets harder to move the higher it climbs.

Fitted against 10,773 driver-sessions from official results: 1.4% error on the predicted CPI, median 0.010 SR, and 93% of final SR values within 0.05. Short races are the least precise, because iRacing counts the distance actually driven while results only report whole laps.

Practice sessions only count when they are part of a Ranked race event. Standalone Open Practice does not affect SR at all.
